BALDUR'S GATE 3
Review

Baldur's Gate 3

RPG

9.9
Masterpiece out of 10
Developer Larian Studios Publisher Larian Studios Platform PC, PS5 Released August 3, 2023 (PC) / September 6, 2023 (PS5) Reviewed On PC

Pros

  • Reactivity to player choices sets a new standard for the genre
  • 12 distinct origin characters, each with unique story perspectives
  • Turn-based combat rewards tactical creativity at every level
  • Extraordinary writing — companion banter alone is worth the price
  • Act 1 alone offers more content than most full-price RPGs

Cons

  • Act 3 has more bugs and pacing issues than earlier chapters
  • Learning curve is steep for players new to D&D 5e rules
  • Some late-game companion arcs feel rushed compared to early ones

The RPG That Raised the Bar

Larian Studios spent three years in Early Access stress-testing Baldur's Gate 3 with the public, and the final release is a game built with obsessive ambition and staggering attention to detail. It adapts the 5th Edition Dungeons & Dragons ruleset with remarkable fidelity while remaining approachable to players who have never touched a tabletop in their lives. More than that, it delivers something the RPG genre has long promised but rarely achieved: a world that genuinely responds to what you do. Not just your major choices — your minor ones. The NPC you pickpocketed in Chapter 1 remembers. The companion you ignored now has opinions about that. The goblin you let live has joined a faction that complicates everything.

Characters as Living People

The companion writing is the finest the studio has ever produced, which is saying something given Divinity: Original Sin II's acclaimed cast. Shadowheart, the guarded half-elf cleric with secrets she's hiding even from herself. Astarion, the vampiric rogue whose dark wit conceals centuries of trauma. Lae'zel, the militant githyanki warrior whose arc quietly becomes the game's most emotionally devastating. Karlach, who arrived in Early Access and immediately became a fan favourite through sheer warmth and fury. These characters feel like real people who change over hundreds of hours — not stat-boosted NPCs who tolerate your presence. The romance writing, in particular, handles intimacy with a maturity and specificity that most games refuse to attempt.

Combat That Rewards Thinking

Baldur's Gate 3's turn-based combat borrows D&D's action economy — one Action, one Bonus Action, movement — and layers environmental interaction on top in ways the tabletop never could. High ground grants attack bonuses; oil barrels can be exploded; enemies can be shoved off ledges; surfaces can be frozen, ignited, or electrified. A fight that looks impossible on paper often has an elegant solution involving lateral thinking. Larian has built a system where creative improvisation is almost always the correct answer, which means even replaying content feels fresh because a different character build opens different tactical doors.

An Imperfect but Defining Achievement

Act 3's Baldur's Gate city — the setting fans of the original duology had waited decades to revisit — buckles slightly under the weight of its ambitions. Quests intersect in ways that occasionally create bugs; some late-game resolutions feel rushed after the deliberate pacing of Act 1. But these are criticisms you raise because the game has set its own standard so high. When Baldur's Gate 3 is at its best — a party-wide conversation resolving a three-act ethical dilemma, a climactic confrontation that plays out differently depending on thirty hours of prior choices, a companion's personal quest reaching a conclusion of genuine dramatic power — it achieves things no RPG before it has managed. It is the genre's new summit.

9.9

Masterpiece — The Definitive RPG of Its Generation

Baldur's Gate 3 sets a new benchmark for reactivity, character writing, and tactical depth in the RPG genre. Its minor stumbles in Act 3 are footnotes beside the scope of what Larian accomplished. Play it, and then play it again differently — you'll be surprised how different differently can be.
