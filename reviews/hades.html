HADES
Review

Hades

Roguelike

9.3
Excellent out of 10
Developer Supergiant Games Publisher Supergiant Games Platform PC, Nintendo Switch, PS4/PS5, Xbox One/Series X|S Released September 17, 2020 Reviewed On PC & Nintendo Switch

Pros

  • Seamlessly integrates narrative progression with roguelike structure
  • Combat is fast, fluid, and endlessly varied across weapon builds
  • Exceptional voice cast brings the Greek pantheon to vivid life
  • Death is a narrative event, not a failure state — genius design
  • Gorgeous hand-painted art style ages like fine wine

Cons

  • Early runs before unlocking upgrades can feel underpowered
  • The Fated List of Minor Prophecies can feel like artificial padding
  • Players averse to repetition will hit a wall before the story pays off

Death as Design

Hades solves the roguelike's fundamental tension — that repetition is the genre's core mechanic and the player's deepest frustration — by making death narratively meaningful. When Zagreus, son of the god of the dead, is slain in his attempts to escape the Underworld, he returns to his father's palace and every character in it remembers exactly what happened. The gods on Olympus who gifted him boons during the run have opinions about his failure. His father Hades, cold and dismissive, has a line that acknowledges the attempt. The skeletal blood-boon shopkeeper shrugs. The architecture of the game is built around the understanding that you will die, often, and those deaths are opportunities for the world to respond.

The Boon System and Combat Diversity

Supergiant gives Zagreus six weapons at the outset — sword, spear, shield, bow, fists, rail gun — each with multiple Aspect variants that fundamentally change how they play. Layer onto these the Boon system, where Olympic gods offer upgrades with distinct flavour and mechanical identity (Artemis focuses on critical hits; Poseidon excels at knockback and terrain hazards; Dionysus inflicts damage-over-time), and the number of viable build combinations is enormous. A run with the Aspect of Guan Yu spear and a full Demeter blizzard build plays nothing like a Coronacht bow run with Apollo harmonics. This diversity ensures that the hundredth escape attempt feels fresh in ways that most games could never sustain.

Greek Mythology, Reimagined

The characterisation in Hades is the best Supergiant has ever achieved — high praise for a studio whose writing has always been a calling card. The voice cast is extraordinary across the board: Darren Korb's dual role as composer and Skelly brings warmth to the most minor character in the game. Logan Cunningham's Hades is imposing and oddly sympathetic. The Olympian gods fizz with personality — Dionysus is a gentle stoner; Aphrodite is aggressively charming; Ares is genuinely intimidating. The story that emerges across dozens of runs — about family, about expectation, about the cycles we inherit — is quietly profound. It sneaks up on you, which is exactly how Supergiant intended.

A Genre Benchmark

Hades did not invent the action roguelike, but it so thoroughly outclasses what came before that it effectively restarted the conversation about what the genre can achieve. Its marriage of narrative investment and mechanical compulsion is so elegant that the game was still selling in enormous numbers two years after launch, on every platform it could reach. The caveat is real: players who bounce off the difficulty of early runs — before the Mirror of Night upgrades add meaningful permanent progression — may not persevere long enough to see the story pay off. If you can push through that initial resistance, what waits on the other side is one of the most satisfying narrative payoffs any roguelike has ever offered.

9.3

Excellent — The Roguelike Reinvented

Hades proves that repetition and storytelling are not in conflict — they can amplify each other. With fluid combat, spectacular variety, and a narrative structure that makes every death meaningful, it is both Supergiant's finest work and the template every roguelike must now reckon with.
