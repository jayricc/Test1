ELDEN RING
Review

Elden Ring

Action RPG

9.8
Essential out of 10
Developer FromSoftware Publisher Bandai Namco Platform PC, PS5, PS4, Xbox Series X/S, Xbox One Released February 25, 2022 Reviewed On PC & PS5

Pros

  • Staggeringly vast open world brimming with secrets
  • Combat system offers unmatched depth and build variety
  • Boss encounters rank among gaming's greatest ever
  • Haunting, collaborative world-building with George R.R. Martin
  • Rewards curiosity, exploration, and patience in equal measure

Cons

  • Late-game reuses bosses more than it should
  • PC performance issues plagued launch and lingered for months
  • Story told so obliquely it risks alienating newcomers

A World Unlike Any Other

There is a moment in Elden Ring — and it comes at a different time for every player — when the game cracks open and reveals the true scale of what FromSoftware has built. For me, it happened when I crested a ridge in Caelid, that blighted red hellscape to the east, and saw a colossal dragon circling a ruins-choked swamp below while two distinct legacy dungeons loomed on the horizon. The Lands Between is not just large; it is layered, interconnected, and alive in a way that no open world had achieved before it.

Combat Refined to Its Purest Form

FromSoftware has been refining their particular brand of weighty, deliberate melee combat for fifteen years, and Elden Ring represents the apex of that philosophy. The introduction of the Spirit Ash summon system, horse combat, and an enormously expanded arsenal of Skills — called Ashes of War — gives players more tools than ever without ever diluting the core tension that defines each encounter. Every fight demands pattern recognition and stamina management; every victory feels genuinely earned. The boss roster is extraordinary. Margit the Fell Omen is an early masterclass in teaching players how to read telegraphs. Radahn is spectacle on a scale games rarely attempt. Malenia — the optional late-game goddess many consider the hardest boss FromSoftware has ever produced — will break you down and rebuild you as a better player, if you let it.

A Mythology Built for the Ages

George R.R. Martin's contribution to the world's backstory is evident in the richness of the Erdtree mythology and the depth of the warring demigod factions you're thrust among. The story is not told to you — it is discovered, pieced together from item descriptions, environmental storytelling, and fragmented NPC monologues. This approach will frustrate players who prefer narrative clarity, but for those willing to engage with it, the Lands Between offers some of the most absorbing lore in modern fantasy. Characters like Ranni the Witch and Blaidd the Half-Wolf have inspired genuine devotion in the game's enormous community, testament to how much personality FromSoftware packs into deliberately sparse dialogue.

A Landmark Achievement

Elden Ring is not a perfect game. The final third recycles bosses in ways that feel lazy compared to the relentless invention of everything preceding them. The PC port launched in a state that required significant patching. But these are grievances filed against a game that has, in every other respect, redefined what an action RPG can be. It synthesizes everything FromSoftware has learned — the intricate level design of Dark Souls, the speed of Bloodborne, the verticality of Sekiro — and unleashes it across an open world that earns comparison with the very best of the genre. Two years after launch, it remains the benchmark against which every ambitious action game is measured. It is, without qualification, a masterpiece.

9.8

Essential — A True Masterpiece

Elden Ring is FromSoftware's magnum opus: a massive, interconnected open world filled with extraordinary encounters, deep lore, and a combat system that remains endlessly satisfying. Minor late-game stumbles cannot diminish what is a landmark in the history of the medium.
