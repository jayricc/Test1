CYBERPUNK 2077
Review

Cyberpunk 2077

Action RPG

8.5
Great out of 10
Developer CD Projekt Red Publisher CD Projekt Platform PC, PS5, Xbox Series X/S (reviewed); PS4/Xbox One (legacy) Released December 10, 2020 Reviewed On PC (post-2.0 update)

Pros

  • Night City is among the most visually dense and detailed cities in gaming
  • Phantom Liberty expansion adds a genuinely great espionage thriller
  • 2.0 patch rework transformed the RPG system and police AI
  • Keanu Reeves's Johnny Silverhand is a standout performance
  • Main story endings carry genuine emotional weight

Cons

  • Launch-era reputation damage and broken promises still linger
  • RPG depth still doesn't match the ambition of its marketing claims
  • Side content quality is wildly uneven — brilliant highs, filler lows

The Comeback Story

Cyberpunk 2077's launch in December 2020 was one of the most disastrous in AAA gaming history. Broken on last-generation consoles, unstable on PC, missing features promised in years of marketing — it was pulled from the PlayStation Store for months. The story of what came after is arguably as compelling as anything in the game itself: CD Projekt Red spent three years patching, reworking, and expanding their game into something that, evaluated today, is genuinely excellent. This review does not forget the launch. But it evaluates the game as it exists now, in the version the developer ultimately intended to ship.

Night City as a World

What was always true, even at launch, is that Night City is a remarkable artistic achievement. CD Projekt Red's worldbuilding team created a city with distinct districts, their own visual identities, their own power structures and street cultures, their own ambient stories told in graffiti and market stalls and overheard conversations. The Corpo megaplexes of City Center feel nothing like the ramshackle neon sprawl of Heywood; the technological utopia of Japantown exists in uncomfortable proximity to the borderline-medieval poverty of Santo Domingo. Walking Night City after the 2.0 update — where police respond intelligently and the city feels genuinely lived in — is one of the best open-world experiences available on current hardware.

V and Johnny Silverhand

The central story — V, a mercenary with a stolen digital ghost sharing their skull, trying to find a cure before the ghost overwrites them — is a vehicle for the game's real interest: the relationship between V and that ghost, Johnny Silverhand, a dead rockstar revolutionary performed by Keanu Reeves with unexpected depth. Their dynamic, antagonistic and eventually grudging and sometimes genuinely warm, carries the game's emotional weight. The branching endings, while fewer than Cyberpunk's scope seems to demand, include at least two that are genuinely affecting. Phantom Liberty adds a fifth, which some consider the best of the lot. CD Projekt Red's writing team, whatever the game's other failings, knows how to close a story.

Still Reaching, Still Great

The 2.0 patch's RPG overhaul — a complete redesign of the perk system, skill trees, and cyberware — addressed the most pointed criticism of the original release: that beneath the gorgeous surface was a shallow action game with RPG window dressing. The reworked systems have genuine depth, though they still fall short of the breadth that games like Baldur's Gate 3 demonstrate is achievable in the genre. The side quest quality remains uneven: the Judy questline is among the best writing in any open-world game; some procedural content barely clears the bar for inclusion. Cyberpunk 2077 in 2024 is not the game it was meant to be at launch, but it is a very good game — occasionally a great one — that has earned its eventual reputation through patient, determined iteration.

8.5

Great — A Redemption Three Years in the Making

Cyberpunk 2077 today is not the game it launched as. Night City is a spectacular achievement, the main story earns its emotional beats, and the 2.0 overhaul finally delivers the RPG depth the original promised. The legacy of a broken launch is real, but so is the quality of what exists now.
