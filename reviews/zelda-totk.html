ZELDA: TEARS OF THE KINGDOM
Review

The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom

Action-Adventure

9.7
Essential out of 10
Developer Nintendo EPD Publisher Nintendo Platform Nintendo Switch Released May 12, 2023 Reviewed On Nintendo Switch OLED

Pros

  • Ultrahand and Fuse mechanics enable extraordinary player creativity
  • Sky islands and Depths add entirely new dimensions to Hyrule
  • Dungeons are far more elaborate than Breath of the Wild's Divine Beasts
  • Narrative is the most emotionally resonant in the series
  • Hundreds of hours of content without a moment that feels like padding

Cons

  • Engine strain causes occasional frame-rate dips on original Switch hardware
  • Weapon durability system returns without meaningful evolution
  • The Depths, while impressive, can feel repetitive in extended sessions

The Impossible Sequel

Following Breath of the Wild — a game that redefined open-world design for an entire generation — was always going to be Nintendo's most daunting challenge. The solution, it turns out, was not to start over but to layer. Tears of the Kingdom takes the same Hyrule, erects a sky archipelago above it, carves a shadow world of Depths beneath it, and then hands the player four new abilities that transform every surface into a potential puzzle, vehicle, or weapon. The result is not merely more of the same. It is a complete reimagining of what the same can mean.

The Joy of Ultrahand

Nintendo's new Ultrahand ability — which allows Link to grab, rotate, and attach almost any object in the world — is one of the great game mechanics of the decade. In theory it sounds like a physics toy; in practice it becomes a lens through which the entire world is recontextualized. A patch of logs near a river is no longer scenery: it's a raft. Three mine carts and a fan are a racing vehicle. Five stakes and a plank become a bridge. The emergent ingenuity players have brought to Ultrahand in the months since launch — rocket-powered contraptions, elaborate traps, flying fortresses — reflects just how deeply Nintendo thought through the system's implications.

Story Worthy of the Legend

The Zelda series has never prioritized narrative complexity, but Tears of the Kingdom's story — told primarily through recovered memories, as its predecessor was — achieves genuine emotional weight. Princess Zelda's arc in particular subverts expectations in ways the game's marketing wisely obscures. The antagonist's motivations are rendered with surprising depth for a series that has historically traded in archetypal evil. When the final act arrives, it earns its spectacle not through visual excess alone but through the story beats that preceded it.

The New Standard for Open Worlds

Tears of the Kingdom's single most remarkable achievement is that it makes a six-year-old game world feel entirely new. Every area of the surface map has been rethought — new towns, new threats, new shrine placements, new environmental storytelling — while the sky and Depths provide wholly distinct experiences. This is not a DLC masquerading as a sequel; it is a fundamentally richer game than the one it follows, running on hardware that is technically outmatched by the ambition on display. Frame-rate compromises are a real cost. But the imagination on display more than justifies them. This is Nintendo at the absolute height of their craft.

9.7

Essential — The Open-World Standard Redefined

Tears of the Kingdom does the seemingly impossible: it makes one of the greatest games ever made feel like a rough draft. Ultrahand transforms exploration into creation, the three-layer world offers hundreds of hours of distinct content, and the narrative finally matches the series' mythic ambitions.
