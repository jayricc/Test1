GOD OF WAR RAGNARÖK
Review

God of War Ragnarök

Action-Adventure

9.5
Essential out of 10
Developer Santa Monica Studio Publisher Sony Interactive Entertainment Platform PS5, PS4 Released November 9, 2022 Reviewed On PlayStation 5

Pros

  • Kratos and Atreus's relationship reaches profound emotional maturity
  • Combat is deeper and more varied than the 2018 predecessor
  • All Nine Realms are realised with stunning visual variety
  • Boss encounters are spectacular and mechanically inventive
  • One of the best-written scripts in PlayStation's history

Cons

  • Pacing bogs down in the middle stretch with too much downtime
  • Some Realm environments feel less developed than others
  • Certain plot threads are resolved too neatly given their weight

The Weight of Fatherhood

God of War (2018) was a reinvention — a raging god learning to be a father, a franchise discovering it had something to say. Ragnarök is the payoff. Santa Monica Studio had four years and the full breadth of Norse mythology to work with, and they spent every hour of it crafting a story about fathers, sons, destiny, and the cost of resistance. The result is among the most emotionally articulate blockbusters Sony has ever published. There are scenes here — quiet ones, between Kratos and Atreus, in the aftermath of violence — that rank among the best the medium has to offer.

Combat Evolved

The Leviathan Axe and Blades of Chaos return, but with expanded movesets, new runic abilities, and a third tool — the Draupnir Spear — that Kratos acquires mid-game and which completely changes his tactical options. Enemies have been redesigned to demand more dynamic responses: shields that require parrying to break, enemies that demand weapon-switching mid-combo, boss patterns that evolve across multiple stages. The result is a combat system that remains accessible to newcomers while rewarding experts who invest time in mastering its considerable depth. Playing on the game's highest difficulty is a genuinely different experience — measured, methodical, almost puzzle-like.

A World of Worlds

The 2018 game was constrained largely to Midgard and brief visits to other realms; Ragnarök opens all Nine Realms and populates each with distinct visual identities, unique enemies, and compelling side content. Vanaheim in particular — a lush, sunlit realm of rivers and ruins — is among the most beautiful environments in a PlayStation exclusive. The Muspelheim and Niflheim endgame challenges provide substantial replay hooks. Even areas that were unavoidably thin — Asgard's brief interior glimpse — are dressed with enough detail to feel like real places rather than designed corridors.

An Imperfect but Remarkable Conclusion

Ragnarök is not a flawless game. Its middle hour loses momentum in extended exposition sequences, and some of its antagonists — despite excellent performances — are not given quite enough screen time to earn the weight the story assigns them. But these are manageable flaws in a game that succeeds at nearly everything it attempts. The combat is the best in the franchise. The story is the most ambitious Sony has ever greenlit for a first-party title. And the final act delivers a Ragnarök worthy of the name — chaotic, devastating, and shot through with hard-won grace. This is how you end a saga.

9.5

Essential — A Stunning End to the Norse Saga

God of War Ragnarök deepens everything its predecessor established: richer combat, grander worlds, and a story that handles its enormous emotional and mythological stakes with remarkable craft. Minor pacing hiccups and the pressure of following a modern classic keep it from perfection, but not by much.
